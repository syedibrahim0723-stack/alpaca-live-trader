# Build the radar from EDGAR, halts, then Benzinga

The best free-first news radar keeps **Alpaca's Benzinga WebSocket as the backbone** and adds sources that Benzinga does not already supply. Those are **SEC EDGAR's "Latest Filings" Atom feed** (filings show up on sec.gov roughly 30–60 seconds after acceptance), the **Nasdaq Trader trade-halt RSS** (updated once a minute, and it covers NYSE-listed names too), **free wire-service RSS** (GlobeNewswire, Business Wire, PR Newswire), and **Finnhub or TickerTick REST polling** for wider ticker coverage. Most "new" vendors repackage what the app already has. Massive (formerly Polygon) sells a Benzinga feed, Interactive Brokers resells Benzinga for $35/month, and Benzinga direct is the same newsroom with webhooks added. More vendors therefore add little; distinct primary sources add a lot. Free general-news APIs are useless for early detection. NewsAPI.org's free plan runs 24 hours behind, and GNews and NewsData.io run about 12 hours behind. The first paid upgrades worth buying are enrichment, not speed: Massive's per-ticker sentiment, StockNewsAPI's analyst upgrade/downgrade feed, and Finnhub's Premium news WebSocket. Speed gains beyond that come from human squawk desks ($35–$400/month, mostly with no API) or institutional feeds (LSEG, Dow Jones, Bloomberg) sold only through sales. One caveat applies to everything below. The research sandbox could not load vendor pricing pages, so **most prices come from search snippets or third-party aggregators and conflict in several places**. No vendor published measured latency numbers. Check every price on the live pricing page before you commit.

## Alpaca already covers most market-data news; three APIs add anything new

Alpaca's news is **sourced directly from Benzinga, has history back to 2015**, and arrives both through a REST endpoint and a real-time WebSocket at `wss://stream.data.alpaca.markets/v1beta1/news` ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/historical-news-data)). Alpaca's own pages give conflicting volume figures: an "average of 130+ articles per day" for history, and 600–900 per day for the stream ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/historical-news-data); [Alpaca news-api](https://alpaca.markets/docs/news-api)). The only published rate limit is from the beta launch post, roughly 2022: **200 calls/min on Free, 10,000/min on Unlimited**. Current limits are unconfirmed ([Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/introducing-news-api-for-real-time-fiancial-news/)). Articles include a `symbols` ticker list but no sentiment. Alpaca's own tutorial runs sentiment through external transformers ([Alpaca Learn](https://alpaca.markets/learn/sentiment-analysis-with-news-api-and-transformers/)).

Of the other market-data APIs, three are worth a look. **Massive** (Polygon rebranded on Oct 30, 2025, and `api.polygon.io` still works) runs its own `/v2/reference/news` feed. That feed has a **per-ticker sentiment label plus a one-sentence rationale from July 2, 2024 onward** ([Massive KB](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/how-far-back-does-massives-news-and-sentiment-data-go); [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/863823068/polygon-io-is-now-massive)). Massive also runs a separate real-time `/benzinga/v2/news` feed that has no sentiment and largely duplicates Alpaca ([Massive KB](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/what-does-massives-benzinga-news-feed-contain)). **Finnhub's** free tier allows 60 calls/min with one year of company news. It is personal and non-commercial only, and one source claims a 300/day cap ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/d/data-providers/finnhub/); [QVeris](https://qveris.ai/guides/stock-api-free-comparison/?lang=zh); [orthogonal.info](https://orthogonal.info/i-tested-4-free-stock-market-apis-heres-which-one-actually-works-for-side-projects/)). Its **news WebSocket is Premium, and its press-release WebSocket is Enterprise-only** ([Finnhub docs](https://www.finnhub.io/docs/api)). **TickerTick** is free for both commercial and non-commercial use, at **10 requests/min per IP**, and covers about 10,000 US tickers over REST ([TickerTick GitHub](https://github.com/hczhu/TickerTick-API)). Commercial use is allowed only on TickerTick, which makes it the safest free breadth source for an app that might ever be monetized.

## Free primary sources beat every free API on speed

The fastest free signal is not a news API. It is the regulator's own feed. EDGAR's `getcurrent` Atom feed can be filtered by form type (8-K, 4, SC 13D) and by insider inclusion ([SEC RSS overview](https://www.sec.gov/files/about/secrss.shtml); [hex.pm parser](https://www.hex.pm/packages/sec_recent_filings_rss_feed_parser/0.0.1)). The SEC says dissemination "usually takes no longer than two minutes" from receipt, and sends to paid PDS subscribers and the website at the same time ([SEC PDS spec](https://www.sec.gov/files/info/edgar/specifications/pds-dissemination-spec070114.pdf)). The only latency measurements are old. A 2015 study of 4,782 Form 4s found a **mean of 40 seconds to public posting, against 30 seconds to Tier 1 subscribers** ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2015/02/fundamental-investors-following-insider-filings-beware/)). The access rules are strict: **at most 10 requests/second**, and a User-Agent header naming the company and a contact email ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data)). Polling a few form types every 2–5 seconds stays far below that cap. Feed entries carry CIK rather than ticker, so the app needs its own CIK-to-ticker map. Avoid the XBRL RSS feeds for real-time use, because they refresh only every ten minutes ([SEC structured data RSS](https://www.sec.gov/structureddata/rss-feeds)). EDGAR is also where many earnings releases appear as 8-K exhibits, so it covers part of the wire's job with a machine-readable issuer ID. That overlap is an inference, not a measurement.

The **Nasdaq Trader halt RSS** (`rss.aspx?feed=tradehalts`) is the only free consolidated halt feed found. It covers Nasdaq-listed and other exchange-listed securities, carries symbol and halt-code fields, updates once a minute, and asks clients **not to poll more than once a minute**. It also requires accepting separate terms ([Nasdaq Trader](https://nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=TradeHaltRSS); [halt codes](https://nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=TradeHaltCodes)). A T1 "news pending" halt is the best free warning that a press release is about to land. NYSE has no free public halt API ([finfeedapi](https://finfeedapi.com/learn/glossary/nyse-api)).

The licensing split matters as much as speed. Government and exchange feeds can be polled within their published rules. Dow Jones/MarketWatch and Seeking Alpha RSS are **explicitly personal, non-commercial**. So a single-user radar showing headlines with links back is low risk, while a multi-user product redistributing full text needs licenses. None of the wires publish how far their free RSS lags their paid partner feeds, and that gap is the largest unknown in the free stack.

## Premium newswires: retail options top out near $400, and most have no API

At retail prices the market splits in two. A few products have self-serve developer APIs: Benzinga (Alpaca, IBKR's $35 add-on, or direct) and NewsAPI.ai/Event Registry, which has ~2,000 free searches and paid plans from about $90/month ([FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/016880/newsapi-ai)). The rest are human squawk and terminal services that the app cannot ingest. **The Fly** runs $35–$65/month ([theflyonthewall.com](https://theflyonthewall.com/news)). **Trade The News** is $360/month standard or **$120 through Lightspeed** ([Lightspeed](https://lightspeed.com/trading-platforms/trader-tools/trade-the-news)). **Newsquawk** is $199 Basic or $399 Pro, with its API enterprise-only ([Newsquawk pricing](https://newsquawk.com/pricing); [Enterprise](https://www.newsquawk.com/features/enterprise)). **Livesquawk** is $350/month ([livesquawk.com](https://livesquawk.com)). **FinancialJuice** has a free tier and paid plans from ~$37/month, but a competitor's comparison describes its license as personal-use only ([TheGodFunded](https://thegodfunded.com/en/tools/financialjuice/); [Newsquawk compare](https://www.newsquawk.com/compare/newsquawk-vs-financialjuice)). Benzinga Pro terminal pricing conflicts between sources: $37/$147/$197 per one site that checked on Oct 9, 2026, against $99/$197/$457 per another ([TheGodFunded](https://thegodfunded.com/en/tools/benzinga-pro/); [Subger](https://subger.com/en/service/benzinga-pro-sub)).

The lowest-latency tier is institutional and priced through sales. **LSEG Headlines Direct** is LSEG's "fastest, lowest latency" headline feed, delivered at its New York trading center. LSEG's Machine Readable News adds JSON full text with sentiment and relevance analytics ([LSEG factsheet](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/data-analytics/en_us/documents/fact-sheets/lseg-headlines-direct-fact-sheet.pdf); [News Analytics](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-analytics/financial-data/financial-news-coverage/political-news-feeds-analysis/news-analytics)). **Bloomberg Event-Driven Feeds** add sentiment, novelty, and "Market Moving News" probability scores. In March 2026 Bloomberg launched customizable tickerised feeds ([Bloomberg EDF](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/data/enterprise-catalog/event-driven-feeds); [Bloomberg press](https://bloomberg.com/company/press/bloomberg-launches-customizable-real-time-news-feeds-for-enhanced-systematic-workflows)). Dow Jones sells an XML-tagged low-latency feed ([WatersTechnology](https://waterstechnology.com/node/1605763)). Accern runs about **$3,000–$7,500/month** ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/B08KWKSX86); [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/d/b2b-services/accern/)). RavenPack/Bigdata.com and AlphaSense do not publish prices ([Bigdata.com](https://bigdata.com/pricing)). Aylien now sits inside Quantexa and appears to be enterprise-only ([NewsAPI.ai comparison](https://newsapi.ai/blog/quantexa-aylien-news-api-alternative-comparison/)). TradingView has no official news API, only unofficial scrapers that likely breach its terms ([Apify](https://apify.com/automation-lab/tradingview-news-scraper)).

The practical consequence: below institutional budgets, **the retail latency floor is the Benzinga WebSocket plus a human squawk on a second screen**. Squawks often voice terminal flashes before text APIs catch up. That is plausible from how they work, but no benchmark was found to confirm it.

## Social feeds are costly or thin, and free news APIs run hours behind

Social media is where tariff posts, CEO tweets, and headline accounts break news, but the access economics changed in 2026. **X dropped its free tier for new developers in February 2026**. Pay-per-use now costs about **$0.005 per post read**, sources disagree on whether the cap is 2M or 3M reads per month, and the filtered real-time stream reportedly requires Pro or Enterprise ([Postproxy](https://www.postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/); [bundle.social](https://bundle.social/blog/x-api-pricing-2026-costs-limits); [OpenTermsArchive](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/x-replaces-its-dollar200-entry-level-api-plan-with-a-pay-per-use-model/)). **xAI's Grok X Search**, which the app already integrates, is reported at $5 per 1,000 tool calls plus tokens ([ai-toolbox](https://ai-toolbox.co/grok-models/grok-pricing-plans-api-2026)). One tracker says billing moved on Sep 21, 2026 to $5 per 1,000 posts plus $10 per 1,000 profiles, which is unconfirmed ([usagepricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/xai-2026-09-21-x-search-per-result-billing)). That makes targeted watchlist sweeps cheap, but the results are sampled and LLM-mediated, not a complete stream. **Trump Media's Truth API** launched Aug 1, 2026, claims millisecond delivery, and was reported at up to ~$100k/month. The third-party **1322.io WebSocket starts at $250/month** ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/truth-api-trump-media/); [t2online](https://t2online.in/tech/tech-news/truth-social-puts-a--100-000-monthly-price-on-hearing-trump-first/2006967); [1322.io](https://1322.io/platforms/truth)). **Unusual Whales** offers flow, alerts, and halts over WebSocket at $750/month (the API alone starts at $150) ([UW enterprise](https://unusualwhales.com/pricing/enterprise); [UW guide](https://unusualwhales.com/guides/best-market-data-api-for-ai-agents)). Bluesky's Jetstream WebSocket is free, but finance discussion there is thin ([Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/blog/bluesky-api-pricing)). StockTwits reportedly removed API access in 2024 ([OneUp](https://help.oneupapp.io/en-us/article/does-oneup-support-stocktwits-1fxjlx0/)). The Reddit API's free tier is non-commercial ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/reddit-api-pricing-in-2026-complete-guide-for-developers-and-businesses/)). **ApeWisdom** gives free mention counts, but its data can be bot-manipulated, so treat it as a hype-velocity signal rather than news ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/d/data-providers/ape-wisdom/)).

General-purpose news APIs are the weakest category for a radar. **NewsAPI.org's free plan is 24 hours delayed and dev-only, and real-time costs $449/month** ([NewsAPI](https://newsapi.org/pricing)). GNews and NewsData.io free tiers run about 12 hours late. Mediastack's free tier allows only 100 calls a month ([socialcrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-google-news-apis-2026); [Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/guides/global-news-api/); [mediastack](https://mediastack.com/pricing)). TheNewsAPI Basic at $19/month is the cheapest paid plan, but its delay is unknown. GDELT is free with no key, returns up to 250 articles per response, and has no ticker tagging ([apidog](https://apidog.com/blog/free-news-api/); [gdeltcloud](https://gdeltcloud.com/compare/news-apis)). These services crawl and re-ingest, so they trail the wires by design.

## Conclusion

The real decision is source diversity, not vendor count. Benzinga sits behind Alpaca, Massive's Benzinga feed, IBKR, and Benzinga direct. Paying for a second Benzinga pipe buys redundancy at best. The independent signals a radar needs (regulatory filings, exchange halts, issuer wires, policy posts) are mostly free or cheap, so a $0 stack can be fast for corporate events as long as the app polls carefully and deduplicates across sources. Spending money mainly buys structure: sentiment, analyst actions, and push delivery. Faster headlines are available only from human squawk desks or six-figure institutional feeds.

Two unknowns should shape the build. First, **no provider publishes measured latency**, and the EDGAR figures date from 2015. The app should log a first-seen timestamp per source for each story so you can rank vendors on your own data before paying for upgrades. Second, licensing and access are tightening. Yahoo's general RSS died in October 2026, X dropped its free tier, and StockTwits and Reddit have narrowed access. Design every connector as replaceable, and prefer government, exchange, and commercial-safe sources (EDGAR, Nasdaq Trader, TickerTick, wires) for anything the app might ever monetize.
